Wow, what a beautiful, moving, and funny documentary. Love simply bursts off the screen. There was so much laughter in the theater, yet by the end, tears were streaming down my cheeks from pure emotion.

What a wonderful childhood the filmmaker must have had with such a lovely father. Even the mother, who plays a small supporting role, radiates that warmth. The decision to ask a filmmaker friend to join the journey makes the film strong; he acts as a kind of alter ego who sharpens the son's perspective and forces him to face the truth, providing some great twists along the way.


## Form and technique 
The staged traffic argument in front of the café terrace was hilarious. From a technical standpoint, there is so much to enjoy: the photo animations are subtle and beautifully executed. I first noticed it in a black-and-white photo during a story about Harry Belafonte, where a lizard crawled across the wall, and later with a train moving between the bars of a balustrade. Truly excellent "cut and paste" work.

The bright orange Renault 4 looks fantastic on screen. The shots through the windshield are gorgeous—I wonder if they were shot on a Sigma fp? What also struck me was the sound of the slamming car doors; it was clearly exaggerated in the mix, but it worked perfectly for the humor in the room.


## Production puzzles
The credits list two DPs, which left me questioning the logistics. While the 'third man' in the film, the friend, is a filmmaker, the credits suggest a different division of labor. For instance, the scene pushing the car across the border was clearly shot by a fourth person. These are the kind of making-of puzzles that stay with you. I'm wondering if there is more staged than I assumed."

Shots through the windshield are gorgeous, feature film like. I wonder what camera they've used. If they were really on the road it could have been a Sigma fp, but if they shot it in a studio, it might have been any camera. 

The scene where the protagonist picks up his friend with the renault: I noticed the slamming car doors and the sound it makes, it is filmed from across the street, so the sound is over exaggerated but works very well. Everyone was laughing. 


## The feeling I went home with

This is the documentary film you want to make, at least: I do.
